Nickel Strip

Nickel Strip Thickness: 0.15, 0.20, 0.30

Pick by current and your spot welder

By Arambhika · 7 November 2026 · 5 min read

Thickness is the quickest way to raise the current a strip can carry, but a thicker strip also needs a more powerful welder. The trade-off decides the choice.

The range

ThicknessTypical useExamples
0.15 mmSmall and mid-current packs, light weldersPlated 10x0.15, pure 10x0.15
0.20 mmHigher-current packsPlated 10x0.20, pure 10x0.20
0.30 mmHighest-current packs, stronger weldersPlated 10x0.30, pure 10x0.30

Width matters too

Current capacity grows with cross-section: width times thickness. A 10 mm wide strip is wider than a 6 mm one at the same thickness, so a 10x0.15 strip can match a 6x0.25 strip. Pick width to suit the cell terminal and the number of weld points.

Your welder sets the limit

Thicker strip needs more weld energy. A small capacitor-discharge welder may weld 0.15 mm easily, struggle with 0.20 mm, and fail on 0.30 mm. Test welds and a pull test tell you more than any rule. See the troubleshooting steps in our weak weld guide.

Do the sum

Work out your continuous and peak current, then use the worked examples in our strip sizing guide. When current is very high, pure nickel or busbar may serve better than a single thick strip.

General guidance only. Check cell, BMS and strip datasheets and the standards that apply to your product before you build or sell a pack.

Frequently asked questions

Is 0.30 mm strip always better?

It carries more current, but it needs a stronger welder and gives more heat at the joint. Use the thickness your current and welder require.

Can I stack two thin strips instead of one thick one?

Some builders do. Test the weld quality, since stacked strip can be harder to weld reliably.

Which is better for e-bikes?

It depends on the motor current and the cells. Many e-bike packs use 0.15 to 0.20 mm strip; high-power builds may need more.
