Build a 12V 100Ah LFP Battery

Four cells, a BMS and busbar, step by step

By Arambhika · 7 November 2026 · 6 min read

A 12.8 V LFP pack of four 100Ah cells holds about 1.3 kWh and weighs far less than a lead-acid battery of similar capacity. Here is the build.

Parts list

  • 4 prismatic LFP cells, 100Ah, from one batch: for example BAK 100Ah or Tianneng 100Ah
  • BMS: Daly LFP 4S 100A smart BMS (or the hardware version)
  • Tin-coated copper busbar: 60 mm bars or the length your cell spacing needs
  • Insulation and a case, with a main fuse and cable of suitable size

Steps

  1. Check the cells. Measure each cell's voltage. Connect them in parallel for several hours so all start at the same level.
  2. Arrange them. Stack the four cells with the compression the maker recommends, with insulation between cells if the cases are conductive.
  3. Connect in series. Link the positive of each cell to the negative of the next with busbar. Torque the bolts evenly.
  4. Add the BMS. Wire the balance leads to the cells in the order the BMS manual shows. Connect the main negative last.
  5. Test. Check the total voltage (about 12.8 V at rest), then run a gentle charge and discharge while watching cell voltages.

Check before you rely on it

Confirm that your inverter or charger can charge lithium within the pack's voltage range. See how to size the wiring in our BMS current rating guide, and why 12 V with high loads draws large current.

General guidance only. Check cell, BMS and strip datasheets and the standards that apply to your product before you build or sell a pack.

Frequently asked questions

Is a 12V 100Ah LFP battery equal to a 100Ah lead-acid battery?

It holds more usable energy, because LFP can be discharged much deeper than lead-acid. Compare usable watt-hours rather than amp-hours alone.

Do I need a special charger?

Use a charger with an LFP profile, or one whose settings you can set to the right voltages for four LFP cells in series.

Is it safe to build my own?

It needs care: clean joints, correct wiring order, proper fusing and a rated BMS. If unsure, use a qualified pack builder.
