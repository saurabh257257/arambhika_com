Copper vs Aluminium Busbar

Current, weight, cost and corrosion compared

By Arambhika · 7 November 2026 · 5 min read

Both metals carry current well and both are used in packs. The better choice depends on how much current you draw, how much weight and cost matter, and how the joints are made.

Side by side

Tin-coated copperAluminium
ConductivityHigherLower, so more cross-section is needed for the same current
WeightHeavierLighter
CorrosionTin coating protects the surfaceOxide forms quickly, so joint prep matters
Joint with large cell terminalsBolted and widely usedBolted or laser welded depending on cell

Our copper options

The universal tin-coated copper bars are 20 mm wide and 2 mm thick, in four lengths: 55 mm, 60 mm, 68 mm and 82 mm. Pick the length that spans your cell-to-cell distance.

Our aluminium options

The Model 1 C2C range covers series, parallel and L-shape links. See the aluminium busbar guide for details.

A simple rule

If current is high, packs are compact and long life matters most, copper is the safe default. If weight and cost lead and the current is moderate, aluminium of enough size can work. Always size the busbar for your continuous and peak current and confirm joint temperature in testing.

General guidance only. Check cell, BMS and strip datasheets and the standards that apply to your product before you build or sell a pack.

Frequently asked questions

Is copper busbar always better?

It carries more current for the same size and resists joint problems well, but it costs more and weighs more. The best choice depends on your design.

Why is copper tin coated?

The coating protects the copper from oxidising and gives a stable surface for bolted joints.

Can I mix copper and aluminium in one pack?

It is possible but dissimilar metals can corrode at the joint in damp air. Avoid direct contact unless the design handles it.
