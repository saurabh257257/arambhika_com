Wiring a Battery Pack Safely

Colour code, crimps, fuses and strain relief

By Arambhika · 23 November 2026 · 5 min read

A pack is only as safe as its wiring. Most wiring faults come from a few habits that are easy to get right.

Colour code

  • Use red for positive and black for negative, everywhere, on every pack. We sell silicone wire in both colours.
  • Never use a red wire for a negative lead, even if it is what you have to hand.
  • Check polarity with a multimeter before you connect a charger or a load.

Keep it short and sized

Short, thick leads waste less power. Choose the gauge from the current and the run length, as worked out in our wire gauge guide.

Crimping

  1. Strip the exact length for the lug or contact.
  2. Use a crimp tool made for the lug. Pliers give a weak, high-resistance joint.
  3. Check that every strand is inside the barrel, and tug firmly on the wire.
  4. Cover the joint with heat shrink so no bare metal shows. See our heat shrink guide.

Fuse the positive

Fit a fuse in the positive lead as close to the battery as you can. It protects the wire from a short in the run. Pick a fuse above your normal current and below the safe current of the wire and connectors.

Strain relief and routing

  • Fix the wire so that a pull is taken by a clamp or a gland, not by a terminal or a weld.
  • Route wires away from sharp edges, strip ends and heat. Use grommets where wires pass through metal.
  • Bundle thin leads with spiral wrap and leave a little slack for vibration.

A final check

With the pack disconnected from its load, check each joint by eye, pull each lug, and confirm continuity with a meter. Then connect and run at a low current before full power.

Wire used for the main path: 7 AWG red and black; for sensor leads: 16 AWG.

General guidance only. Check cell, BMS and strip datasheets and the standards that apply to your product before you build or sell a pack.

Frequently asked questions

Where does the fuse go?

In the positive lead, as close to the battery as possible.

Can I twist wires instead of crimping?

A twisted joint can heat and loosen. Use a crimped lug or a soldered, insulated joint.

Why does strain relief matter?

A pull on the wire can tear a weld or loosen a terminal. A clamp or gland takes the load instead.
