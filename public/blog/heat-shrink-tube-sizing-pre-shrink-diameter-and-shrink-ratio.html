Heat Shrink Tube Sizing

Pre-shrink size, ratio and a quick check

By Arambhika · 23 November 2026 · 5 min read

A heat shrink tube has to do two things: slide over the part before heating, and grip it after. Two numbers decide both.

The two numbers

  • Pre-shrink diameter is the size of the tube as supplied, which is the size in the listing. The tube must slide easily over the largest part it has to cover.
  • Recovered diameter is the size after full shrinking. It must be smaller than the smallest part, so the tube grips it.

The shrink ratio links the two. Most heat shrink tube is 2:1, so it shrinks to about half its supplied diameter. Check the ratio of your tube, since some are 3:1 and others are lower.

A worked example

A 10 mm tube at 2:1 recovers to about 5 mm. It slides over a part up to a little under 10 mm and grips parts down to about 5 mm or slightly more. So it suits a joint that is about 6 to 9 mm across. A part of exactly 10 mm will not slide on, and a 4 mm wire would leave the tube loose.

Check before you buy

  1. Measure the biggest part the tube must pass over, such as a crimped lug barrel, with a vernier caliper.
  2. Measure the smallest part it must grip, such as the bare wire insulation.
  3. Pick a tube whose supplied size is a little larger than the first and whose recovered size is smaller than the second.
  4. Test on a scrap piece first.

Frequently asked questions

What is a 2:1 shrink ratio?

The tube shrinks to about half its supplied diameter.

Can I use one tube size for all wires?

No. Match the tube to the part it covers. Too small will not fit, too large will stay loose.

Does heat shrink replace tape?

It insulates a joint neatly. Tape and heat shrink do different jobs, and both are useful.
