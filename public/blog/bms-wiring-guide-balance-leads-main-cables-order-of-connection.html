How to Wire a BMS Correctly

Balance leads first, main cables last

By Arambhika · 7 November 2026 · 5 min read

Most BMS failures on new builds come from wiring mistakes, not faulty boards. A careful order of work avoids them.

Before you start

  • Read the manual for your exact board; wiring varies by model.
  • Make sure the cells are already joined in series and parallel and that total voltage is as expected.
  • Remove rings and use insulated tools.

Step by step

  1. Identify the taps. The balance wire harness has one wire at the pack negative, then one after each series group, ending at the pack positive.
  2. Measure before plugging. With a multimeter, check each tap in turn against the lowest tap. The voltage should step up by one cell voltage (about 3.2 V for LFP, 3.6 V for NMC) at each wire.
  3. Connect the balance plug. Plug in the harness only after the check passes.
  4. Connect the main negative. From the pack negative (B-) to the BMS, following the diagram.
  5. Test outputs. Check voltage on the output terminals before attaching a load.
  6. Attach the load and charger only after the board shows normal cell readings.

Common mistakes

  • Wires in the wrong order, which can destroy the board.
  • Thin main cable, causing heat; use copper sized for the current, such as copper busbar for short links.
  • Missing fuse on the main positive.

Boards to practise on

A 4S smart BMS is a simple first board, and the app shows you at once if a cell is wired wrongly.

General guidance only. Check cell, BMS and strip datasheets and the standards that apply to your product before you build or sell a pack.

Frequently asked questions

What do B-, P- and C- mean?

Labels differ by board. Typically B- goes to the pack negative, P- to the load and C- to the charger on a separate-port board. Check your manual.

Can I plug the balance lead in live?

Do the voltage check first and connect only if everything is right. Following the manual order reduces risk.

Do I need a fuse if there is a BMS?

Yes. The BMS does not replace a correctly rated fuse or breaker.
