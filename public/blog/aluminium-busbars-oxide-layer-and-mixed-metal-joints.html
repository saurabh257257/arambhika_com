Aluminium Busbar Joints

Oxide, cleaning and mixing metals safely

By Arambhika · 23 November 2026 · 5 min read

Aluminium is light and cheap, but its surface behaves differently from copper. A little care at the joint pays off.

The oxide film

As soon as aluminium is exposed to air it forms a very thin, hard oxide layer. This layer protects the metal underneath, but it does not conduct well. A joint made over a thick or dirty oxide layer has higher resistance and runs hotter than it should.

Preparing a joint

  1. Clean the mating faces of dirt and grease with a lint-free cloth and isopropyl alcohol.
  2. If the surface is visibly dull, remove the oxide just before assembly with a fine abrasive pad, in one direction, and wipe off the dust.
  3. Assemble soon after cleaning, since the oxide begins to re-form in minutes.
  4. Use the washers and torque recommended for the stud, and re-check the torque after the first heat cycles.
  5. For welded joints, follow the welder's cleaning and shielding guidance, since contamination causes weak welds.

Where aluminium meets other metals

When two different metals touch in a damp environment, the more reactive one corrodes faster. This is called galvanic corrosion. Aluminium is more reactive than copper, tin and steel, so an aluminium busbar bolted to a copper terminal can corrode at the joint in a humid place such as a coastal area or a monsoon-exposed enclosure.

  • Keep joints dry, and seal the enclosure against moisture.
  • Use hardware made for the combination, such as plated washers, and avoid mixing metals where you can.
  • Inspect joints periodically for white powder, discolouration or heat marks.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to sand aluminium before bolting?

Clean it first. If the surface is dull, a fine abrasive pad removes oxide just before assembly. Do not leave it for hours.

Can aluminium touch copper?

It can, but in damp conditions the joint can corrode. Keep it dry and use suitable hardware.

Why does a joint get warm?

A high-resistance joint, from oxide, dirt or low torque, turns current into heat. Clean it and check the torque.
