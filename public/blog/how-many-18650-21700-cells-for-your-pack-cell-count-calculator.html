Cell Count for Your Pack

Series, parallel and total cells, worked out

By Arambhika · 23 November 2026 · 5 min read

Give yourself a target voltage and capacity, and three lines of arithmetic tell you how many cells to buy.

The three steps

  1. Series count (S) = target voltage divided by 3.6 V, the nominal voltage of an NMC cell. Round to the nearest whole number.
  2. Parallel count (P) = target capacity in Ah divided by the cell capacity in Ah. Round up.
  3. Total cells = S times P. Energy in Wh = S x 3.6 x P x cell Ah.

Example 1: a 48 V, 20 Ah pack from 21700 cells

With 4500 mAh 21700 cells:

  • S = 48 / 3.6 = 13.3, so use 13S (46.8 V nominal).
  • P = 20 / 4.5 = 4.4, so use 5P, which gives 22.5 Ah.
  • Total = 13 x 5 = 65 cells. Energy = 46.8 V x 22.5 Ah = about 1053 Wh.

Example 2: a 36 V, 10 Ah pack from 18650 cells

With 2600 mAh 18650 cells:

  • S = 36 / 3.6 = 10, so 10S.
  • P = 10 / 2.6 = 3.8, so 4P, which gives 10.4 Ah.
  • Total = 10 x 4 = 40 cells. Energy = 36 V x 10.4 Ah = about 374 Wh.

Then check three things

  • Current. Peak pack current divided by P must stay below the cell's continuous rating. See how to read the spec.
  • Full-charge voltage. S x 4.2 V is the top voltage your charger and BMS must handle.
  • Buy spares. Order a few extra cells for test welds and for any cell that fails your receiving check.

For a full build see Build a 13S 48V 21700 Pack, and use a BMS with the matching series count (see this guide).

General guidance only. Check cell, BMS and strip datasheets and the standards that apply to your product before you build or sell a pack.

Frequently asked questions

Why 3.6 V per cell?

It is the nominal voltage used for NMC cells. Some datasheets use 3.7 V, which changes the numbers slightly.

Should I round up or down for the series count?

To the nearest whole number, then check the full-charge voltage (S x 4.2 V) against your motor controller and charger.

Do I need extra cells?

Order a few more than the total, for test welds and rejects at the receiving check.
