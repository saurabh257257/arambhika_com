Smart BMS: UART, RS485, CAN

Which communication port you actually need

By Arambhika · 23 November 2026 · 5 min read

Smart BMS listings are full of acronyms. Each port solves a different problem, and many packs need only one of them.

What each one does

PortTypical use
BluetoothCheck voltages, current and temperatures on your phone, and change settings, without a cable.
UARTA simple serial link for a PC or display, and for the BMS maker's own monitoring tools.
RS485A longer, more robust serial link, often used to read several batteries on one bus.
CANA bus that lets the BMS tell a compatible inverter or charger what charge voltage and current to use.

Which do you need?

  • One pack and you want to look at it. Bluetooth with the maker's app is enough.
  • Several batteries in parallel. RS485 lets one device read them all, if your monitor supports it.
  • A hybrid solar inverter that talks to the battery. The inverter needs to support lithium communication, usually over CAN or RS485, and the BMS must speak the same protocol.

Check compatibility before you buy

Two devices having the same kind of port does not mean they understand each other. The messages on that port follow a protocol, and inverters list which battery brands and protocols they support. Ask the inverter maker for the compatible BMS list, and check that the BMS model offers the matching protocol.

If your inverter has no communication with the battery, you do not need CAN or RS485. You just set the inverter's charge voltage by hand to suit the pack and let the BMS protect it, as covered in BMS for a 24V inverter and UPS.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need CAN or RS485 for a normal e-bike pack?

No. Bluetooth or no communication is usually enough. The wired ports matter when a device has to read or control the battery.

Will any CAN BMS work with my inverter?

No. The inverter must support the BMS protocol. Check the inverter maker's compatibility list.

Can I monitor several batteries with Bluetooth?

The app normally connects to one BMS at a time. For many batteries on one screen look at RS485 and a monitor that supports it.
