Prismatic Cell Compression

Why LFP cells need clamping

By Arambhika · 7 November 2026 · 5 min read

Prismatic LFP cells grow slightly as they charge and age. A pack that ignores this can lose life or loosen joints.

Why cells swell

Inside, the electrodes change volume with state of charge, and over time gas and ageing add a little more. The cell cans flex, especially the large faces. Packs without restraint let the cell bulge, which can affect cycle life.

Clamping the stack

  1. Find the compression range the maker gives for the cell (a force or a pressure).
  2. Use rigid end plates and threaded rods or straps to hold the stack within that range.
  3. Put a flat insulating separator between cells where cans are conductive, for example epoxy sheet.
  4. Re-check tightness after the first cycles.

Do not over-tighten

Too much force is as bad as none. Keep within the stated range and use the same compression on every cell.

Which cells need it?

All large prismatic cells, such as the 314Ah and 280Ah. Follow the datasheet of the model you use. See the 314Ah build for assembly order.

General guidance only. Check cell, BMS and strip datasheets and the standards that apply to your product before you build or sell a pack.

Frequently asked questions

Is a plastic case enough?

Not unless it is designed to hold the stack under the required compression.

Do 100Ah cells need compression?

Check each model's datasheet; smaller cells may have lighter requirements.

Can I use foam between cells?

Some designs do. Choose a material that gives even pressure and does not creep away over time.
