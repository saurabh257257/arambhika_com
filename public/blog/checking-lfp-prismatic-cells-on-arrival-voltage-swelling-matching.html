Checking LFP Cells on Arrival

A ten-minute inspection before you build

By Arambhika · 23 November 2026 · 5 min read

Ten minutes with a multimeter when a box arrives saves hours of trouble later. Here is a short routine.

Before you open the box

  • Check the outer packaging for crush damage, wet patches or burn marks.
  • Count the cells against the invoice, and note the model and batch labels.
  • Keep a photo of the box and labels in case of a claim.

Look at each cell

  • Shape. Faces should be flat. A swollen or bulging cell is rejected, not repaired.
  • Case and vent. No dents, cracks, leaks or residue around the vent.
  • Terminals. Clean, flat and undamaged, with the studs or weld surfaces unmarked.
  • Labels. Same model, capacity and batch or date code on every cell.

Measure and record

  1. Let the cells rest at room temperature, then measure each cell at the terminals with a meter you trust.
  2. LFP cells are usually shipped partly charged, so expect a resting voltage somewhere around 3.2 to 3.3 V. The datasheet or supplier will give the expected value.
  3. Write every voltage down. Cells in one pack should be close together. Builders usually top-balance cells to the same voltage before connecting them in parallel or series, as covered in Top Balancing LFP Cells.
  4. If you have an internal resistance meter, record that as well. A cell that reads very differently from its neighbours should be questioned.

If something is wrong

Do not charge a damaged or very low cell. Photograph it, stop, and contact us the same day with the batch details so it can be sorted out. A fault found at the receiving stage is far easier to resolve than one found after the pack is welded together.

Frequently asked questions

What voltage should new LFP cells read?

They are normally shipped partly charged, so around 3.2 to 3.3 V is typical. Check your cell datasheet for the exact range.

Should I charge cells before building?

Top-balance cells to the same voltage before you join them, following the cell datasheet and a charger suited to LFP.

What should I do with a swollen cell?

Do not use it. Keep it away from heat and other cells and send us photos and the batch details.
