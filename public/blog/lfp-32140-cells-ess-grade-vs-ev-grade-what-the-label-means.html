ESS vs EV Grade 32140 Cells

Match the label to what your pack does

By Arambhika · 23 November 2026 · 5 min read

Cell labels such as "ESS A grade" or "EV" describe the intended use. They are not a promise on their own, so here is how to read them.

What the labels usually say

  • ESS stands for energy storage system. Stationary packs for solar, home backup and telecom are charged and discharged slowly, so the cell is chosen for capacity, cycle life and consistency.
  • EV points to mobility use, such as e-bikes and light vehicles. These packs see higher current and vibration, so the cell's discharge rating matters more.
  • A grade is a supplier's way of saying the cell passed grading for capacity and resistance. Different suppliers grade differently, so it is a starting point for questions, not a standard.

Match the cell to the job

Your packWhat to prioritise
Solar or inverter backupCapacity match, cycle life, low self-discharge
E-bike or light EVContinuous and peak discharge current, resistance, cell strength
Telecom or UPSReliability and a tight capacity match across cells

Ask for the datasheet

Before a bulk order, ask for the datasheet of the exact model and check:

  1. Nominal capacity and voltage (3.2 V, 15 Ah for most 32140 cells).
  2. Charge and discharge limits in amps.
  3. Cycle life, and the conditions it was tested at.
  4. Size and weight, so your holders and enclosure fit.
  5. Internal resistance, and whether cells in a batch are matched.

For example, the CNAE 32140 ESS A grade is described for energy storage, and the CBAK 32140 EV is listed for e-bikes, light EVs and storage. If you are unsure which to use, send us your load and pack voltage on WhatsApp.

Frequently asked questions

Is an EV grade cell better than an ESS cell?

Neither is better in general. They are chosen for different jobs, so match the datasheet to your load.

What does A grade guarantee?

On its own, not much. Ask the supplier what test the grade is based on and for the datasheet.

Can I use ESS cells in an e-bike?

Only if the continuous and peak discharge ratings cover your load. Check the datasheet.
