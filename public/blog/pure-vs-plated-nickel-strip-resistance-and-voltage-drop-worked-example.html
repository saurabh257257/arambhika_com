Nickel Strip

Pure vs Plated: The Numbers

Resistance, heat and voltage drop worked out

By Arambhika · 23 November 2026 · 5 min read

People say pure nickel conducts better than plated strip. Here is by how much, using one simple formula.

The formula

Resistance of a strip is its resistivity times its length, divided by its cross-section: R = ρ x L / A. Use metres for length and square metres for the area.

Handbook resistivity at 20 °C: pure nickel about 6.99 x 10-8 ohm metre. Mild steel, the base of plated strip, is roughly 1.4 to 1.7 x 10-7 ohm metre. We use 1.43 x 10-7. The plating is only a few microns thick, so the steel dominates the plated strip.

Example 1: 8 x 0.15 mm, 100 mm long

Cross-section is 1.2 mm2, or 1.2 x 10-6 m2.

  • Pure nickel: 6.99e-8 x 0.1 / 1.2e-6 = about 5.8 milliohm
  • Plated steel: 1.43e-7 x 0.1 / 1.2e-6 = about 11.9 milliohm

At 20 A the heat in the strip is I2R. That is about 2.3 W for pure nickel and 4.8 W for plated. The voltage drop is about 0.12 V against 0.24 V.

Example 2: 10 x 0.30 mm, same length

Cross-section is 3 mm2.

  • Pure nickel: about 2.3 milliohm
  • Plated steel: about 4.8 milliohm

The same current now makes about 0.9 W and 1.9 W. Doubling the cross-section halves the resistance, and choosing pure nickel halves it again.

What to take from this

  • Pure nickel has about half the resistance of plated strip of the same size, so it runs cooler at the same current.
  • You can reach the same resistance with plated strip by making it thicker or wider, at a different price per kg. Compare both options for your current.
  • These figures are for the strip itself. The weld spots add their own resistance, and so does the cell terminal.

Pure strips used in the examples: 1P 8x0.15 mm and 1P 10x0.30 mm. For a wider comparison see Pure Nickel Strip for High-Current Packs.

General guidance only. Check cell, BMS and strip datasheets and the standards that apply to your product before you build or sell a pack.

Frequently asked questions

Are these resistance values exact?

They are calculated from handbook resistivities. Real strip varies with alloy, temperature and tolerance, so use them to compare options, not as specifications.

Does thicker pure nickel always run cooler?

For the same width and length, yes: resistance falls as the cross-section rises. The weld joints still matter.

Is it worth paying more for pure nickel?

It is worth it when current is high, space is tight or heat is a problem. For light loads, plated strip is usually enough.
