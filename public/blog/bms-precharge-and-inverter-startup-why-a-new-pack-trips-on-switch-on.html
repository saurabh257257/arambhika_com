BMS Trips at Switch-On

Inrush current and pre-charge, explained

By Arambhika · 23 November 2026 · 5 min read

The pack tests fine, the BMS is rated for the current, and yet it shuts off the instant you connect the inverter. The cause is usually a brief surge, not a fault.

What happens at switch-on

Inverters and motor controllers have large capacitors across their DC input. When you connect an empty capacitor to a battery, it draws a very large current for a few milliseconds while it charges. The BMS sees this as a short circuit and cuts the output, because to the BMS it looks like one.

Signs it is inrush, not a fault

  • The trip happens at the moment you connect or flip the switch, before any load is running.
  • It happens with the inverter or controller, but not with a small test load.
  • Once the BMS output is on, the system runs normally.

Three fixes

  1. Pre-charge. Charge the capacitors through a resistor first, then close the main connection. A pre-charge resistor in series with a switch, or a BMS with a pre-charge function, limits the surge. The resistor value and the time must suit the capacitance and voltage, so use the inverter maker's recommendation or the BMS manual.
  2. Check the short-circuit setting. Smart BMS models let you adjust the delay of the short-circuit trip in their app. A very short delay trips on a brief surge. Do not disable protection, only tune the delay within the limits in the manual.
  3. Pick a higher rating. A BMS with more headroom in its peak current can ride out a small surge. It does not remove the need to limit the inrush for a large inverter.

Do not bypass the BMS

Connecting the inverter straight to the cells to get past the trip removes your protection and can arc at the terminals. Solve the cause instead. If you are unsure, send us the inverter model, the pack voltage and the BMS you have.

Frequently asked questions

Is a trip at switch-on dangerous?

It is the BMS doing its job. The surge is real, so fix it with pre-charge or settings rather than removing the protection.

What is a pre-charge resistor?

A resistor that limits the current while the input capacitors charge. After a short time a main switch closes and the resistor is bypassed.

Will a bigger BMS fix it?

Sometimes, if the surge is small. For a large inverter the inrush should still be limited.
