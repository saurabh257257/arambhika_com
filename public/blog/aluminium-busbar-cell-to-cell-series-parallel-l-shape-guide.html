Aluminium Busbar Guide

Series, parallel and L-shape cell links

By Arambhika · 7 November 2026 · 5 min read

Aluminium busbars are a lightweight, low-cost way to link large prismatic cells. The Model 1 C2C range comes in three shapes, each for a different connection.

The three shapes

BusbarUse
Model 1 C2C SeriesLinks the positive of one cell to the negative of the next, building voltage
Model 1 C2C ParallelJoins like terminals of cells side by side, building capacity
Model 1 C2C L ShapeTurns a corner or reaches the pack output where a straight bar will not fit

Prepare the joint

  1. Clean the cell terminal and the busbar contact face, removing oil and the dull oxide layer.
  2. Fit the busbar flat so the whole contact area touches the terminal.
  3. Tighten to the torque the cell maker specifies, and mark each bolt once done.
  4. Re-check after the first few charge cycles, since joints can settle.

Why aluminium needs care

Aluminium forms an oxide film quickly, and when it touches a different metal in damp air it can corrode. If your cell terminal is a different metal, use the hardware the cell maker recommends and keep the joint dry and tight. Your design and the cell datasheet decide the right combination.

When to choose copper instead

Aluminium carries less current than copper of the same size. For high-current packs see the copper vs aluminium comparison and our tin-coated copper busbar guide.

General guidance only. Check cell, BMS and strip datasheets and the standards that apply to your product before you build or sell a pack.

Frequently asked questions

What does C2C mean?

It stands for cell to cell: the busbar is designed to connect one cell to another.

Can I weld aluminium busbar to the cell?

Some prismatic cells are laser welded, others are bolted. Follow the cell maker for the method it supports.

Do I need anti-oxidation paste?

Many builders use a contact compound on aluminium joints. Follow the cell and busbar maker's guidance for your build.
