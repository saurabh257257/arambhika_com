Series and Parallel Explained

The simple maths behind every pack label

By Arambhika · 7 November 2026 · 5 min read

Every pack label hides a bit of arithmetic. Once you know it, you can design any battery.

The rules

  • Series (S) adds voltage. Capacity stays the same.
  • Parallel (P) adds capacity. Voltage stays the same.

So a 13S4P pack of 4.5 Ah cells has 13 x 3.6 = 46.8 V and 4 x 4.5 = 18 Ah. Energy is voltage times capacity: 46.8 x 18 = 842 Wh. Cells needed: 13 x 4 = 52.

Working backwards

  1. Choose the voltage the motor or inverter needs, which fixes S.
  2. Choose the energy you want, divide by voltage to get Ah.
  3. Divide by the cell's Ah to get P.
  4. Check current per cell against its rating.

Connect it

Spot-welded packs link cells with strip such as 2P strip; prismatic packs use busbar. Cells: Norda 21700. See the 13S4P build.

General guidance only. Check cell, BMS and strip datasheets and the standards that apply to your product before you build or sell a pack.

Frequently asked questions

What does 16S1P mean?

16 cells in series with one cell per group, so one string of 16.

Does adding parallel cells increase voltage?

No. Parallel cells increase capacity and current capability, not voltage.

How do I find the cells needed?

Multiply S by P. A 13S4P pack needs 52 cells.
