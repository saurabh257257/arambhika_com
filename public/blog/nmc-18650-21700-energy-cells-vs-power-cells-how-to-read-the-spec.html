Energy vs Power NMC Cells

What the capacity and current ratings tell you

By Arambhika · 23 November 2026 · 5 min read

Two 18650 cells can look the same and behave very differently. The numbers on the datasheet tell you which is which.

The five numbers that matter

SpecWhat it tells you
Capacity (mAh)How much energy is stored. More mAh means longer runtime.
Max continuous discharge (A)The current the cell can supply for long periods without overheating.
Internal resistance (milliohm)Lower is better. It sets heat and voltage sag under load.
Cycle lifeHow many full cycles to a stated capacity, at stated currents and temperatures.
Charge currentThe safe standard charge rate. Fast charging needs a cell rated for it.

Energy cells and power cells

An energy cell is built to store as much as possible, for slow, steady loads such as a power bank, a light or a battery for a laptop-style drain. A power cell is built to deliver high current, so it usually has a lower capacity for the same size but handles tools or e-bike bursts with less heat.

Choosing for your load

  1. Work out the peak current of your pack, then divide by the number of cells in parallel. That is the current per cell.
  2. Compare it with the maximum continuous discharge rating. Stay comfortably below it.
  3. Within the cells that pass, pick the capacity that gives the runtime you want.

For example, a pack that peaks at 40 A with 4 cells in parallel asks 10 A from each cell. A cell rated for a lower continuous current will get hot there.

Where to find the numbers

Always read the manufacturer datasheet for the exact model and batch, not just the label. The ratings differ by maker, by model and by test conditions. Frequently asked questions

Is a higher mAh cell always better?

Only if the load is light. High-capacity cells often have a lower maximum current, so check the discharge rating first.

What does EV grade mean?

It is a supplier term for a cell intended for e-mobility use. Ask for the datasheet to see the actual ratings.

Can I mix cells from different makers?

No. Keep one model and one batch in a pack, so capacity and resistance are matched.
