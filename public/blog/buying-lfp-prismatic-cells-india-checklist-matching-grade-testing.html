Buying LFP Cells: A Checklist

Grade, batch matching and incoming tests

By Arambhika · 7 November 2026 · 5 min read

Cells are the largest cost in a pack, and a poor batch ruins everything built on top of it. These checks reduce the risk.

Ask before you order

  • The maker and model, and the datasheet for that model.
  • The grade being sold (for example A-grade or ESS grade) and what that means for the supplier.
  • Whether all cells come from one production batch.
  • Terminal type: bolted, laser-weld, and whether busbars are included.

Know the listings

For large cells, see the Highstar 314Ah and CALB 280Ah; for mid-size, BAK 100Ah; for round cells, Mocean 32140. Pack variants such as "with packing" or "laser welding" change what you receive, so read each listing.

Tests on arrival

  1. Visual. Check for dents, leaks or damaged terminals.
  2. Voltage. Measure every cell at rest. A wide spread suggests mixed batches or poor storage.
  3. Internal resistance. Compare cells with the same meter; outliers should not share a pack.
  4. Capacity. Run a controlled charge-discharge on a sample, or all cells if the pack is expensive.

Match, then build

Group cells by voltage and resistance, top-balance them in parallel, and only then assemble. Pair with a suitable BMS from the series count guide, and follow the 314Ah build guide or the mid-size cell guide.

General guidance only. Check cell, BMS and strip datasheets and the standards that apply to your product before you build or sell a pack.

Frequently asked questions

What does A-grade mean?

It is the supplier's grading term and is not a single universal standard. Ask for the datasheet and the test data behind the grade.

Should I test every cell?

For expensive or critical packs, yes. At minimum, measure the voltage and internal resistance of every cell and capacity-test a sample.

Can I mix cells bought at different times?

Avoid it. Different batches and ages age differently, which causes balancing problems. Build the whole pack from one batch.
