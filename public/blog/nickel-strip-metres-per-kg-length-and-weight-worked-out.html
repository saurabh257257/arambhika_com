Metres per Kilo of Nickel Strip

Length and weight for every common size

By Arambhika · 23 November 2026 · 5 min read

Nickel strip is sold by the kilo, but a pack is built in metres. Here is how to convert one into the other.

The one-line formula

A strip that is 1 mm2 in cross-section and 1 metre long has a volume of exactly 1 cm3. So the weight of one metre in grams is the cross-section in mm2 times the density of the metal in g/cm3. Cross-section is width times thickness.

Plated strip is a steel base with a nickel layer, so we use about 7.85 g/cm3. Pure nickel is about 8.9 g/cm3. These are handbook values, so treat the results as close estimates, not as a guarantee for a particular coil.

A worked example

You need 25 m of plated 1P 8x0.15 mm. At about 106 m per kg that is roughly 0.24 kg. Our minimum order for these strips is 3 kg, which is about 318 m of that size, so a single order covers a lot of packs.

Using it to compare strips

Because pure nickel is denser, the same size weighs about 13 percent more per metre than plated. Compare strips by price per metre as well as price per kg, especially when you are choosing between sizes. The price per metre is the price per kg divided by the metres in a kilo.

The 2P strips for 18650, 21700 and 32650 cells are formed shapes, so the table above does not apply to them. For those, count strips, not metres.

General guidance only. Check cell, BMS and strip datasheets and the standards that apply to your product before you build or sell a pack.

Frequently asked questions

Are these weights exact?

No. They come from standard densities and the nominal size. Real coils vary slightly with tolerance and plating thickness, so use the figures for planning.

Why does a thicker strip give fewer metres per kg?

Weight per metre grows with cross-section, so a strip with twice the cross-section gives about half the length from the same weight.

How much do I have to order?

The minimum order is shown on each product page. For most strips it is 3 kg.
