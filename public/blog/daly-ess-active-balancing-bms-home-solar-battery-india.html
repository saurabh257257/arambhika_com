Active Balancing BMS for Home Solar

Why large LFP storage packs need it

By Arambhika · 7 November 2026 · 5 min read

Large home storage batteries run for years and hold big cells, so tiny differences between cells add up. Active balancing moves charge between cells instead of burning it off as heat. Here is when it is worth paying for.

Passive vs active balancing

A passive balancer bleeds the highest cells through small resistors until the rest catch up. It is simple and cheap, but the energy is lost as heat and the balancing current is small. An active balancer transfers energy from stronger cells to weaker ones, so balancing is faster and wastes little.

Why large cells make this matter

On big prismatic cells such as 280 Ah and 314 Ah, a small voltage difference can mean a large amount of charge to move. A bleed-only balancer may take a long time to correct it, especially if the pack rarely reaches full charge. That is common in solar systems that cycle in the middle of the range.

Match it to the inverter

Check three things before you order: the series count matches your cells, the continuous current covers your inverter's peak draw, and the communication port suits your inverter. Pair the BMS with matched cells, for example the 314Ah 16S build, and bolted busbar.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need active balancing for a small battery?

Not usually. For small packs with few cells, normal balancing is often enough. Active balancing pays off on large cells and long-life storage.

Is 15S or 16S better for solar storage?

Both are used. 16S gives about 51.2 V and is widely used with 48 V class inverters. 15S gives about 48 V. Follow your inverter manufacturer's recommended range.

Will the BMS balance during discharge?

That depends on the model and its settings. Check the specification of the exact board you choose.
