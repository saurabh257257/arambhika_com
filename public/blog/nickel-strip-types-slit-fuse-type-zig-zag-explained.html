Slit, Fuse and Zig-Zag Nickel Strip

What each strip design is for

By Arambhika · 7 November 2026 · 5 min read

Beyond plain strip, we sell shaped strips for particular packs. Each design solves a specific build problem.

Plain strip (1P)

Straight strip in widths such as 6, 8 and 10 mm, in several thicknesses. You cut it to length and weld it where you need it. It is the most flexible option, for example plated 8x0.15 or pure 8x0.15.

Slit and no-slit 2P strip

2P strip is pre-formed for two cells, and some versions come with a slit cut in the strip while others do not. See the pairs: 18650 without slit and 21700 with slit. The right choice depends on your welder and layout, so test a few joints before you buy in bulk.

Fuse-type strip

A fuse-type strip has a narrowed section between cells so that, if one cell draws a fault current, the narrow section is designed to open. It adds a layer of protection at cell level. Examples are plated fuse type and pure fuse type. It does not replace the main fuse or the BMS.

Zig-zag strip

Zig-zag strip is formed in a repeating shape for layouts that need it, such as the pure 18650 zig-zag. Send us your layout if you are not sure it suits your pack.

Plated or pure?

Pure nickel conducts better than plated steel-core strip, so the same size handles more current. Plated is the lower-cost route. See the plated vs pure price guide and the CD guide to finish selecting.

General guidance only. Check cell, BMS and strip datasheets and the standards that apply to your product before you build or sell a pack.

Frequently asked questions

Does a fuse-type strip replace a BMS?

No. It adds cell-level protection in certain faults, but the BMS and main fuse are still required.

Are slit strips easier to weld?

It depends on your welder and settings. Run test welds on both before committing to a batch.

Can I get a custom shape?
