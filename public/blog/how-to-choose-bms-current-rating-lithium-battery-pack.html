BMS Guide

Choosing a BMS Current Rating

Continuous, peak and the safety margin

By Arambhika · 7 November 2026 · 5 min read

The most common BMS mistake is an undersized current rating. A board that trips under load, or runs hot all day, is usually too small for the job. The sum is simple.

The formula

Current in amps equals power in watts divided by pack voltage. Use the peak power your motor or inverter can draw, not the label figure for normal running, then add a margin for start-up surges and heat.

Worked examples

LoadPackCurrentBMS to consider
1.5 kW inverter12.8 V (4S LFP)about 117 AHigher than 100 A, so use a larger board or a 24 V pack
1.5 kW inverter51.2 V (16S LFP)about 29 AA 60 A or 100 A board has comfortable margin
2 kW e-scooter motor48 V (15S LFP)about 42 A15S 100A keeps margin
5 kW motor73.6 V (23S LFP)about 68 A23S 100A

The figures are simple power divided by nominal voltage. Real current rises as voltage sags, so leave headroom.

Why a bigger BMS is not wasteful

A BMS running near its limit warms up and ages faster. Choosing the next rating up costs little compared with a nuisance trip on a hill or a cloudy-day inverter surge. For 12 V packs with heavy loads, compare the 4S 100A with a higher voltage design. The universal 7S-24S 100A board also suits lower-power packs of many sizes.

Do not forget the wiring

The BMS rating means little if the strip or busbar between cells overheats first. Check our strip sizing guide as well.

General guidance only. Check cell, BMS and strip datasheets and the standards that apply to your product before you build or sell a pack.

Frequently asked questions

How many amps BMS do I need for 1000W?

At 48 V, 1000 W is about 21 A, so a 40 A to 60 A board gives margin. At 12 V the same load is about 83 A, which needs a 100 A class board or more.

Is the peak or continuous rating more important?

Both. Choose the continuous rating for your normal load and check that the BMS peak rating covers motor start-up or inverter surge.

Can two BMS be used in parallel?

Do not parallel BMS boards on one pack unless the manufacturer says it is supported. Use a single higher-rated board instead.
