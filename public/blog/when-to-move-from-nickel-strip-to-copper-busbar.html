Nickel Strip or Copper Busbar?

A simple test for when strip is not enough

By Arambhika · 23 November 2026 · 5 min read

Nickel strip suits small cylindrical cells. Copper busbar suits large cells and high current. Between them lies a grey zone, and this is how to cross it.

The cell decides first

  • Small cylindrical cells (18650, 21700). The terminals are designed for spot-welded nickel strip. A bolted busbar has nothing to bolt to.
  • Large prismatic cells. These have bolt-on or weld-on terminals sized for busbars. Nickel strip is not the normal connection.
  • Large cylindrical cells (32650, 32140). Thicker nickel strip is common, but at high current builders also use busbars on a bolted design.

Current is the second test

A strip that is 10 mm wide and 0.30 mm thick has a cross-section of 3 mm2. A 20 x 2 mm copper bar has 40 mm2, and copper conducts better than nickel. If your pack current needs more conductor than a few strips in parallel can give, move to copper. Run the numbers with our strip sizing guide and the busbar planning guide.

Other things that tip it

FactorNickel stripCopper busbar
JoiningSpot or laser weldBolted (or welded for some designs)
RepairHard: cut and re-weldEasy: unbolt and replace
ToolsSpot welderTorque wrench
VibrationGood welds hold wellNeeds correct torque and washers

Rule of thumb

If your cells are small cylinders and the current per parallel group is modest, use nickel strip. If your cells are large with bolted terminals, or the current needs more conductor than strip can reasonably give, use copper. Not sure? Tell us the cell and the pack current on WhatsApp.

Examples: copper 60x20x2 mm and pure nickel 1P 10x0.30 mm.

General guidance only. Check cell, BMS and strip datasheets and the standards that apply to your product before you build or sell a pack.

Frequently asked questions

Can I bolt a busbar to an 18650 cell?

No. Those cells have no bolt terminal. They are joined with spot-welded strip.

Is copper always better than nickel?

It conducts better, but the right choice depends on the cell terminals, the current and how you assemble the pack.

Which is cheaper?

It depends on the size and the number of joints. Compare the full set of parts for your pack, not just the per-piece price.
